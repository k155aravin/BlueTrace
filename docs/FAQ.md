# BlueTrace FAQ

## What does BlueTrace do?

BlueTrace scans for nearby Bluetooth devices and helps you notice if the same unknown device appears across multiple locations.

## Does BlueTrace prove someone is following me?

No. BlueTrace shows awareness signals, not proof. Bluetooth signals can be noisy, randomized, blocked, repeated, or misread.

## What does Watch mean?

Watch means an unknown device appeared at two scan points. It deserves attention, but it is not an alert by itself.

## What does Alert mean?

Alert means an unknown device appeared at all three scan points. This is the strongest pattern BlueTrace currently looks for, but it is still not proof of identity or danger.

## Why does Android ask for Location permission?

Android requires location-related permission for Bluetooth Low Energy scanning. BlueTrace uses it to scan nearby Bluetooth signals, not to create a map of your location.

## Does BlueTrace upload my Bluetooth data?

No. The current BlueTrace prototype stores trusted devices and baseline data locally on the phone.

## What are trusted devices?

Trusted devices are devices you own or recognize. BlueTrace ignores them during security sweeps.

## What is the baseline profile?

The baseline profile is a quiet-place reference. It helps BlueTrace learn what is normal around you so familiar background devices do not keep appearing as suspicious.

## Can I use BlueTrace while driving?

Use Driving mode only when it is safe. Do not interact with the app while operating a vehicle. Have a passenger operate the app, or stop safely before scanning.

## What should I do if BlueTrace shows Alert?

Stay calm. Review the device details, consider running another sweep, and use your judgment. If you believe you are in immediate danger, leave the area and contact local emergency services.
